# LEGAL FRAMEWORK FOR SOFTWARE PROTECTION (PERU & ANDEAN COMMUNITY)

This document describes the current legal framework in the Republic of Peru and the member countries of the **Andean Community (CAN)** — Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador and Peru — that supports the protection of intellectual and industrial property rights of **SportMatch Connect**. It includes references to applicable national and international regulations, as well as current administrative fees according to the Single Text of Administrative Procedures (TUPA) of INDECOPI.

---

## 📜 1. Legislative Decree No. 822 — Copyright Law (Peru)

Software (logical support) is assimilated under the category of **literary works** and is regulated under the following guidelines:

*   **Article 2 (Definition):** *Logical Support (Computer Program)* is defined as the expression of a set of instructions through words, codes, plans, or in any other form, which, when incorporated into an automated reading device, is capable of causing a computer to perform a task or achieve a result. It also includes technical documentation and user manuals.
*   **Article 3 (Automatic Inception):** Copyright protection is born with the creation of the work, independent of its merit, destination, or registration formalities. No formality is required for the existence of the right.
*   **Article 5 (Content of the Right):** Copyright comprises moral rights (paternity, integrity, disclosure, and withdrawal from the market) and economic rights (reproduction, distribution, public communication, translation, adaptation, arrangement, and transformation).
*   **Article 16 and 23 (Ownership in Works Made for Hire / Employment Relationship):** Unless agreed otherwise, it is presumed that the economic rights (commercial exploitation) of software developed in the academic or organizational scope under employment are transferred to the employer or applicant (in this case, Universidad San Ignacio de Loyola - USIL), while the students retain their inalienable **moral rights** (recognition of authorship and integrity of the work).
*   **Article 16 (Original Ownership):** Unless proven otherwise, the author is presumed to be the natural person whose name or pseudonym appears on the work. In the case of SportMatch Connect, the five co-authors (Flores, Andrade, Espinoza, Gastelu, Salvatierra) are the original owners of both moral and economic rights, unless the latter are assigned to USIL.
*   **Article 17 (Work of Collaboration):** When a work is created by two or more persons, the economic rights belong to all co-authors in the proportion they determine. For SportMatch Connect, it is recommended to establish a written agreement setting the contribution percentage of each co-author (e.g., 20% each).
*   **Article 18 (Collective Work):** A collective work is one created on the initiative of a natural or legal person who coordinates and publishes it under their name. If USIL sponsors and coordinates the project, SportMatch Connect could qualify as a collective work, with USIL holding the economic rights.
*   **Article 23 (Assignment of Economic Rights):** Economic rights may be assigned by written agreement. The assignment covers only the rights expressly indicated and for the agreed term and territory. The assignment letter for SportMatch Connect must specify: (a) assigned rights (reproduction, distribution, public communication), (b) term of assignment, (c) territory (Peru and/or international).
*   **Article 24 (Limitations on Assignment):** The assignment of economic rights over future works is null and void. Therefore, the assignment of SportMatch Connect can only refer to the current registered version, not to future versions or derivatives.
*   **Article 28 (Right of Reproduction):** The holder of economic rights has the exclusive right to authorize or prohibit the reproduction of the work by any means or procedure, including temporary or permanent storage in digital media.
*   **Article 31 (Right of Distribution):** The holder has the exclusive right to authorize distribution to the public of the original or copies of the work through sale, rental, public lending, or any other transfer of ownership.
*   **Article 34 (Right of Public Communication):** It includes any transmission or dissemination of the work to the public by any means or procedure, including making it available on the Internet (which protects the web deployment of SportMatch Connect).
*   **Article 37 (Right of Transformation):** The holder may authorize or prohibit translation, adaptation, arrangement, or any other transformation of the work. This protects the source code of SportMatch Connect against unauthorized modifications.
*   **Article 40 (Computer Programs — Scope of Protection):** Computer programs are protected under the same terms as literary works. Protection includes source code, object code, technical documentation, user manuals, and the graphical interface. The reproduction of a backup copy and the observation, study, or verification of the program's functioning to determine underlying ideas and principles are permitted.
*   **Article 41 (Leasing of Computer Programs):** The author or owner of a computer program has the exclusive right to authorize commercial leasing of the program or its copies, even after the first sale.
*   **Article 58 (Related Rights — Phonogram Producers):** Protects producers of sound recordings, applicable if SportMatch Connect includes voice recordings or ambient sounds on its platform.
*   **Article 78 (Common Provisions — Registration):** The registration of works and related rights with INDECOPI is declaratory and not constitutive. However, registration produces the following effects: (a) certainty of the work's date, (b) legal presumption of authorship, (c) enforceability against third parties.
*   **Article 79 (Content of the Register):** The following are recorded in the register: (a) constitutive, declaratory, modifying, or extintive acts of copyright; (b) protected works; (c) assignments and licenses; (d) encumbrances and seizures; (e) any other act or contract that modifies the ownership of the right.
*   **Article 80 (Effectiveness of Registration):** Acts registered in the National Copyright Register are enforceable against third parties in good faith. Registration does not validate null or voidable acts under the law.
*   **Article 83 (Administrative Appeals):** Against resolutions of the Copyright Directorate, appeals for reconsideration and appeal are available. The appeal is filed before the INDECOPI Tribunal.
*   **Article 89 (Infringements):** Any act of exploitation of a protected work without the authorization of the rights holder constitutes an infringement, including unauthorized reproduction, distribution, public communication, and transformation.
*   **Article 90 (Provisional Measures):** The rights holder may request provisional measures, including: (a) cessation of the infringing activity; (b) seizure of unlawful copies; (c) temporary closure of the infringing establishment; (d) suspension of hosting or web hosting services.

### Registration before INDECOPI
Registration of software with the **Copyright Directorate (DDA)** of INDECOPI is **declaratory** (not constitutive) in nature. It provides certainty of date and legal presumption of authorship in case of judicial controversy. It is carried out using form **F-DDA-02** (Registration of Logical Support / Computer Program) and payment of the corresponding administrative fee under code **203000707**, set at **S/ 390.50** according to the current TUPA of the Banco de la Nación (fee schedule updated as of 01.01.2025 and Resolution No. 000003-2026-PRE/INDECOPI).

### Application Analysis to the SportMatch Connect Project

**Case study: Ownership and rights of the academic team**

In the academic context of USIL, the development of SportMatch Connect is carried out as a final degree project. According to DL 822, Arts. 16 and 23:

1. **Authors (inalienable moral rights):** Edwin Flores Sánchez, Paolo Andrade Noa, Erick Espinoza Mayta, Matías Gastelu Ponte and Juan Salvatierra Ramirez are the authors of the source code, architecture, and documentation. They retain perpetually and inalienably the moral rights: paternity (that their names appear on the work), integrity (that the work is not mutilated or deformed), disclosure (deciding whether and how it is published) and withdrawal (suspending exploitation due to change of convictions).

2. **Assignment of economic rights:** If the project is developed under the institutional framework of USIL using university resources (laboratories, software licenses, servers), the economic rights are presumed to correspond to USIL, unless otherwise agreed in writing. It is recommended that the team sign an **Assignment of Economic Rights Deed** specifying:
   - Assignment limited to version 1.0 of SportMatch Connect.
   - Duration of the assignment (recommended: 5 years renewable).
   - Royalty percentage for the authors (e.g., 30% distributed equally among the 5 members).
   - Right of authors to use the code in future non-commercial projects (personal projects, portfolio, thesis).

3. **Protection of the name and trademark:** The name "SportMatch Connect" is not protected by copyright registration. It is also recommended to register the **trade name** or **trademark** with INDECOPI (Directorate of Distinctive Signs) to prevent third parties from using the name.

## 📜 2. Decision 351 of the Andean Community (Common Regime on Copyright)

Establishes a uniform transnational protection within the member countries (Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, and Peru), with supranational rank and direct application:

*   **Article 3:** Logical support is protected under the same terms as literary works. Protection extends to both human-readable instructions (source code) and machine-executable instructions (object code).
*   **Article 4 (Nationality):** Authors who are nationals of any member country and works first published in CAN territory are protected.
*   **Article 11 (Economic Rights):** Holders of economic rights may authorize or prohibit reproduction, distribution to the public, public communication, and transformation of their works.
*   **Article 52 (Minimum Duration):** The minimum duration of protection is the life of the author plus 50 years after their death, but Peru has extended this term to 70 years (DL 822, Art. 52), which prevails as it is more favorable to the author.

### Detailed Analysis of Additional Articles of Decision 351

*   **Article 4 (Scope of Protection):** Protection covers national authors of the Member Countries (Bolivia, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru), foreigners domiciled therein, and works first published in CAN territory, regardless of the author's nationality. SportMatch Connect is automatically protected in all four Andean countries.
*   **Article 5 (Protected Works):** Among the protected works are expressly included "computer programs (logical support)" as an autonomous category, reinforcing that software does not need to be assimilated to literary works — it is a category of its own with express protection.
*   **Article 6 (Requirements for Protection):** Protection is automatic and independent of the merit, purpose, or form of expression of the work. No registration or formality of any kind is required for software to be protected in CAN countries.
*   **Article 8 (Moral Right of Paternity):** The author has the irrevocable and inalienable right to: (a) keep the work unpublished or disclose it; (b) claim authorship of the work at any time; (c) oppose any deformation, mutilation, or modification that harms the work or the author's reputation. For SportMatch Connect, this means that no team member may be excluded from credits without their express consent.
*   **Article 9 (Moral Right of Integrity):** The author may oppose any unauthorized modification of the work, even after assignment of economic rights. If USIL or a third party modifies the code of SportMatch Connect in a way that distorts the original creation, the authors may demand the cessation of such modification.
*   **Article 10 (Right of Disclosure):** The author decides whether and in what form their work is disclosed. The SportMatch Connect team must collectively agree on the publication of the GitHub repository, the public deployment of the platform, and any academic or commercial presentation.
*   **Article 12 (Right of Reproduction):** The holder may authorize or prohibit direct or indirect, temporary or permanent reproduction, by any means and in any form, of all or part of the work. This includes downloading the code, local installation, and server storage.
*   **Article 13 (Right of Distribution):** The holder may authorize or prohibit distribution to the public of the original or copies through sale, rental, lease, or public lending. The first sale exhausts the distribution right only for that specific copy, not for the work as a whole.
*   **Article 14 (Right of Public Communication):** Includes making the work available to the public by wire or wireless means, so that any person may access it from the place and at the time they choose ("making available" right). This article protects the web deployment of SportMatch Connect on public servers.
*   **Article 15 (Right of Transformation):** Includes translation, adaptation, arrangement, or other modifications. Any fork or modification of the SportMatch Connect code requires authorization from the rights holder.
*   **Article 20 (Duration of Moral Rights):** Moral rights are perpetual, inalienable, irrevocable, and imprescriptible. After the author's death, their exercise corresponds to their heirs or the competent authority.
*   **Article 21 (Duration of Economic Rights for Natural Persons):** The entire life of the author plus 50 years after their death (minimum; countries may extend it, as Peru did to 70 years).
*   **Article 22 (Duration for Collective Works):** 50 years counted from the first authorized publication or from its creation if not published within that period. For SportMatch Connect as a collective work (if USIL is the holder), patrimonial protection lasts 50 years from publication.
*   **Article 58 (Interpretation and Application):** Member Countries may not apply more restrictive rules than those established in Decision 351, but they may apply more favorable rules to authors. This explains why Peru was able to extend the term from 50 to 70 years.

## 📜 3. Decision 486 of the Andean Community (Common Regime on Industrial Property)

Regulates access to invention patents throughout the Andean territory, published in the Official Gazette of the Cartagena Agreement No. 600 (September 14, 2000):

*   **Article 14 (Patentability Requirements):** Inventions that are **novel**, involve an **inventive step**, and are susceptible of **industrial application** shall be patentable. The invention may relate to a product, a process, or a use.
*   **Article 15, literal e) (Exclusions from Patentability):** Computer programs or logical support, **"as such"** (i.e., isolated from hardware or a technical effect in the physical world), are not considered inventions.
*   **Article 16 (Exceptions to Patentability):** Inventions whose commercial exploitation must be prevented to protect public order, health, or life of persons or animals, or to preserve plants or the environment, are also not patentable.
*   **Articles 18-19 (Inventive Step and Industrial Application):** An invention is considered to involve an inventive step if, to a person skilled in the art, it is not obvious nor does it derive in an evident manner from the state of the art.
*   **Boundary Line for CII (Computer-Implemented Inventions):** The examination guidelines of the **Directorate of Inventions and New Technologies (DIN)** of INDECOPI, in accordance with Decision 486, establish that if a computer program does not operate in isolation, but controls a physical process, transforms external electromagnetic signals, or interacts with physical hardware achieving a **measurable and novel technical effect**, it qualifies as a **Computer-Implemented Invention (CII)** and is eligible for patent protection. In SportMatch Connect, the following elements comply with these guidelines:
    *   Predictive geospatial matchmaking algorithm that transforms GPS coordinates (real-world physical variables) into a numerical compatibility score.
    *   Image classification processing through convolutional neural networks on the client device, reducing bandwidth consumption and network latency.
    *   Atomic financial transactions protected by Row Level Security that guarantee the integrity of the digital wallet system.

### Detailed Analysis of Additional Articles of Decision 486

*   **Article 15 (Exclusions from Patentability — Complete List):** In addition to computer programs "as such" (literal e), the following are not considered inventions: (a) discoveries, scientific theories, and mathematical methods; (b) purely aesthetic creations; (c) plans, rules, and methods for the exercise of intellectual activities, games, or economic-commercial activities; (d) forms of presenting information. SportMatch Connect overcomes these exclusions because its technical effect goes beyond mere information processing.
*   **Article 17 (Sufficient Disclosure):** The patent application must disclose the invention in a manner sufficiently clear and complete for a person skilled in the art to carry it out. For SportMatch Connect, the descriptive report must include flow diagrams, pseudocode of the Haversine, Elo, and Gale-Shapley algorithms, and the architecture of the Edge AI moderation system.
*   **Article 20 (Claims):** The claims define the subject matter of the protection. They must be clear and concise and be supported by the descriptive report. For SportMatch Connect, the claims should be drafted in three categories: (a) system claims, (b) method claims, (c) computer program product claims.
*   **Article 21 (Unity of Invention):** The patent application must relate to a single invention or to a group of inventions linked so that they form a single general inventive concept.
*   **Article 22 (Priority):** The applicant may claim priority from an earlier application filed in another WTO member country or Paris Convention country, within 12 months of the first filing. This allows SportMatch Connect to file first in Peru and then in other countries.
*   **Article 23 (Right of Priority):** The priority claim must be submitted within 16 months from the priority date, accompanied by a certified copy of the priority application.
*   **Article 24 (Formal Examination):** The competent national authority (DIN of INDECOPI) examines whether the application meets the formal requirements within 30 days from filing.
*   **Article 25 (Publication):** The patent application is published 18 months from the filing date or claimed priority date. During this period, the application is confidential.
*   **Article 26 (Third-Party Observations):** Any person may submit reasoned observations to the patent application within 6 months following publication. This could be a disadvantage if competitors oppose the SportMatch Connect patent.
*   **Article 27 (Substantive Examination):** Substantive examination must be requested within 6 months following publication of the application. If not requested within this period, the application is deemed abandoned.
*   **Article 28 (State of the Art Report):** The competent authority prepares a state-of-the-art search report that serves as the basis for substantive examination.
*   **Article 29 (Patentability Requirements — Novelty):** An invention is new when it is not included in the state of the art. The state of the art comprises everything that has been made accessible to the public before the filing or priority date.
*   **Article 30 (Exception to Novelty Due to Disclosure):** Novelty is not destroyed if the disclosure occurs within 12 months prior to the filing date and was the result of: (a) an unlawful act against the applicant, (b) an exhibition at an official or recognized fair, (c) a publication by the applicant within the previous 12 months. This provides a safety margin for academic presentations.
*   **Article 31 (Inventive Step):** An invention is considered to involve an inventive step if, to a person skilled in the art, it is not obvious nor does it derive in an evident manner from the state of the art.
*   **Article 32 (Industrial Application):** An invention is considered susceptible of industrial application when its subject matter can be produced or used in any type of industry.

#### 6.3. Relevant International Precedents

1. **Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank (USA, 2014):** The U.S. Supreme Court established the two-step "Alice test" for determining software patentability: (1) determine whether the claim is directed to an abstract idea, (2) if so, determine whether it contains an "inventive concept" sufficient to transform it into a patentable application. This test is cited by the DIN of INDECOPI as an interpretive reference.

2. **Decision G 1/19 of the European Patent Office (EPO, 2020):** Established that computer simulation of a technical process may be patentable if it produces a technical effect. This precedent is relevant for the SportMatch Connect patent, which simulates and optimizes sports matchmaking in the real world.

3. **Viacom v. YouTube (USA, 2012):** Established limits to the liability of digital platforms for user-uploaded content. Relevant for content moderation in SportMatch Connect.

#### 7.3. Procedural Recommendations for the SportMatch Connect Team

1. **Order of procedures:** Register the copyright first (S/ 390.50, fast and secure), then file the patent application (S/ 720.00, longer and more complex process).
2. **Confidentiality:** Keep the code repository private (GitHub Enterprise or private GitLab) until after filing the patent application so as not to destroy novelty.
3. **Legal advice:** For the patent, hire a lawyer specialized in Peruvian industrial property with experience in CII. The DIN of INDECOPI is very rigorous in examining claims.
4. **Critical deadlines:** Do not miss the deadline to request substantive examination (6 months from publication). If it expires, the application is automatically abandoned.
5. **Academic disclosure:** If the project is to be presented at conferences or fairs, do so after filing the patent application, or within the 12-month grace period of Art. 30 of Decision 486.
